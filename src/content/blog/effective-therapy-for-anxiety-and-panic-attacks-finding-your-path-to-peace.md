## Understanding the Full Experience of Anxiety and Panic

Anxiety isn't just racing thoughts or worry—it's a whole-body experience that affects every aspect of your life. You might notice your chest tightening during meetings, your muscles tensing before important conversations, or that overwhelming sense of losing control when stress peaks. These aren't just random sensations; they're your nervous system's response to perceived threats, even when those threats aren't physically present.

My approach recognizes that anxiety involves the complex interplay between your biology (how your nervous system functions), your psychology (thoughts, beliefs, and emotions), and your social world (relationships, work environment, and culture). This biopsychosocial framework means I don't just focus on managing symptoms—I help you understand the deeper connections that create and maintain anxiety patterns.

Physical symptoms often mirror serious medical conditions, which can be frightening. That racing heart, shortness of breath, or dizziness during a panic attack can feel like a medical emergency. Understanding that these are your body's protective mechanisms, though uncomfortable, helps reduce the fear that often perpetuates the anxiety cycle.

### Identifying Your Unique Triggers and Patterns

Everyone's anxiety has its own signature. Yours might spike around performance reviews, family gatherings, or when facing uncertainty in relationships. Sometimes triggers aren't obvious—they can be subtle reminders of past experiences or accumulated stress that suddenly overflows.

I work with you to map these patterns, examining not just what triggers anxiety but how different parts of you respond. This deeper understanding becomes the foundation for lasting change. For instance, if you're navigating intense work environments or startup life, we'll explore how those specific pressures interact with your personal history and current relationships to create your unique anxiety experience.

## Internal Family Systems (IFS): Understanding Your Inner World

Have you ever felt like different parts of you are in conflict? Maybe one part pushes you to achieve more while another part feels exhausted and wants to retreat? Internal Family Systems therapy recognizes that we all have different "parts" or sub-personalities, each trying to help in its own way.

In my practice, I help you understand these protective parts—the perfectionist who fears criticism, the controller trying to prevent chaos, or the worrier attempting to keep you safe from disappointment. These parts aren't enemies to defeat; they're protective strategies that once served you well but may now be creating anxiety.

Through IFS, you'll learn to access your core Self—that calm, confident, compassionate essence that can lead your internal system with wisdom. When your protective parts trust your Self to handle challenges, they can relax their extreme strategies. This creates internal harmony where anxiety transforms from an overwhelming force into a manageable messenger.

The process involves developing genuine curiosity about these parts, understanding their positive intentions, and helping them find new, less extreme ways to protect you. This shift from internal conflict to internal collaboration is transformative, especially for those who've spent years fighting against their anxiety.

## Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT): Living Fully Despite Anxiety

ACT offers a refreshing perspective: instead of exhausting yourself trying to eliminate anxiety, you learn to change your relationship with it. Think of it as learning to sail through a storm rather than waiting for perfect weather that may never come.

The core of ACT is psychological flexibility—your ability to stay present and engaged with what matters most, even when anxiety shows up. This doesn't mean being happy all the time; it means building the skills to handle whatever life presents while still moving toward your goals.

I help you identify your deepest values—what truly matters beyond achievement and external success. Then we work on taking values-aligned action even when anxiety is present. This might mean having that difficult conversation with your partner despite feeling nervous, or pursuing a meaningful project even when self-doubt creeps in.

Key components we work with include:

- **Cognitive defusion**: Learning to see thoughts as just thoughts, not absolute truths

- **Acceptance**: Developing willingness to experience difficult feelings without struggling

- **Present-moment awareness**: Using mindfulness to stay grounded in the here and now

- **Values clarification**: Identifying what gives your life meaning and purpose

- **Committed action**: Taking consistent steps toward what matters, even when it's challenging

## Relational Life Therapy (RLT): Transforming Relationship Dynamics

Anxiety doesn't exist in isolation—it profoundly affects how we connect with others. Whether you're married, preparing for marriage, or navigating relationship challenges, anxiety can create patterns that push loved ones away or create unhealthy dependencies.

RLT helps you see these patterns clearly and develop new ways of relating. Instead of withdrawing when stressed or seeking constant reassurance, you'll learn to communicate authentically and build genuine intimacy. This involves expressing needs clearly without demanding, listening to truly understand your partner's perspective, and knowing how to repair relationships after conflicts.

I take a direct approach, sometimes challenging unhelpful patterns that keep you stuck. The goal is creating relationships where both people feel safe, seen, and valued—relationships that support growth rather than perpetuate anxiety cycles.

## Addressing Work Stress and Preventing Burnout

Having witnessed the intensity of corporate life and startup environments firsthand, I understand the unique pressures you face. The constant demands, the need to be "always on," and the pursuit of excellence can create a perfect storm for anxiety and eventual burnout.

Work-related anxiety often stems from multiple sources: overwhelming workload, lack of control, insufficient recognition, challenging workplace relationships, or misalignment between your work and values. I help you identify your specific stressors and develop sustainable strategies that don't compromise your career trajectory.

Setting boundaries isn't about being difficult—it's about self-preservation and maintaining long-term effectiveness. We'll work on practical implementation: defining work hours and sticking to them, communicating limits clearly, scheduling genuine downtime, and learning to say no gracefully when necessary.

Preventing burnout requires more than just stress management techniques. It involves reclaiming your energy through meaningful self-care, reconnecting with activities that bring joy outside work, and building a rhythm that allows peak performance without depletion. Together, we'll create an approach where your work supports your overall well-being rather than consuming it.

## Processing Grief, Trauma, and Life Transitions

Major life changes—whether losses, relationship transitions, or new responsibilities—can trigger or intensify anxiety. These experiences shake our sense of stability and force us to adapt to new realities. Even positive changes like promotions or starting a family can bring unexpected anxiety.

My approach to processing difficult experiences isn't about forgetting or "moving on" quickly. It's about acknowledging impact, creating safe space for emotions, and gradually integrating these experiences into your life story without letting them dominate your present. Past wounds don't have to define your future, but they do need acknowledgment and careful processing to truly heal.

For those navigating major transitions, I provide strategies for adjustment: setting realistic expectations about the adaptation process, building supportive structures, and maintaining self-care when life feels chaotic. The goal is helping you move through change with greater resilience and self-compassion.

## Building Genuine Self-Worth from Within

So much anxiety stems from tying self-worth to external validation—the next promotion, positive feedback, or relationship success. When these external markers shift or disappear, confidence crumbles. This creates an exhausting cycle of achievement-chasing that never quite satisfies.

I help you build a more solid foundation of self-worth that comes from within. This involves recognizing your inherent value separate from achievements, developing genuine self-compassion, and learning to trust your inner wisdom. Real confidence isn't about never feeling doubt—it's about trusting yourself to navigate challenges even when uncertainty is present.

We work on noticing and internalizing accomplishments rather than dismissing them, challenging your inner critic with curiosity rather than judgment, and taking actions aligned with your values rather than external expectations. This shift from external to internal validation is transformative for managing anxiety and building lasting resilience.
